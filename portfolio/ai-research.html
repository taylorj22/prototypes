Context

Adding AI to Roofr was already decided

By the time this research started, AI was already in motion: Growth had an AI Receptionist in beta, an AI Proposal Builder was planned, and more was coming. The question wasn't whether to build AI features: it was how to release them in a way that drives adoption instead of resistance.

This research gives every team designing and shipping AI a shared, evidence-based foundation. It's generative: we're not testing feature ideas, we're discovering real mental models. The output shapes how AI features are released, named, designed, and explained, not just which ones to build.

Two failure modes to design around

Competitive analysis surfaced a tension every competitor is navigating:

  • Over-transparency: labeling everything "AI-powered" triggers skepticism in contractors who associate AI with inaccuracy or gimmicks. The label can be a barrier before they've even tried the feature.
  • Under-transparency: hiding AI use erodes trust when customers eventually discover it. In a high-stakes industry where an estimate error costs real money, contractors want to know what's generating the numbers they're signing off on.

The research was designed to find where Roofr's customers sit, and how that varies across segments.

Phase 1 combined three methods to build a foundation before going deeper:

  • Competitor audit: Map what AccuLynx, JobNimbus, Hover, ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro, and XBuild are doing with AI. Identify trust patterns and messaging strategies.
  • Industry survey: 182 completed responses from Roofr customers (active 1+ months, any plan). Segmented by AI Readiness, not just company size. 55% owners or founders, decision-makers, not just users.
  • Generative interview pipeline: survey opt-ins screened against AI Readiness segments for Phase 2 depth interviews.

AI Readiness segmentation

Treating our customer base as one group would miss the critical nuance. The segmentation framework was a core design decision:

Current Users 44
Actively using AI regularly or have tried a few tools. Most optimistic. Already experimenting in estimates and admin.
AI-Curious 52
Haven't actively sought out AI but are open to it, or tried something and stopped. Largest opportunity segment, need a reason and a low-risk entry point.
Accidental Users 18
Not seeking AI, but already using features in existing software without thinking of it as AI. Can be converted without needing to sell AI explicitly.
Skeptics 30
Not interested and not using AI. Core concerns: accuracy, customer relationships, data privacy. Will need proof before consideration.

Competitor audit: key patterns

XBuild Emerging / AI-native
AI-native estimating: describe the job in plain language, get a complete estimate and proposal in minutes. Integrates Roofr measurements as input. Most urgent competitive threat, free-to-start lowers the trial barrier.
Outcome-focus: "Close the deal before you leave the driveway"
ServiceTitan Indirect
Most aggressive AI in the space. "Atlas," an agentic co-pilot across the full platform. Customers report double-digit revenue growth attributed to the AI suite.
Enterprise credibility + measurable ROI claims
Jobber Indirect
Jobber Voice (hands-free field commands), AI Quote Automations, Campaign Generator. Also has an AI receptionist add-on.
Removing friction from physically demanding work
Hover Direct / Measurement
Hover AI: measurements, design, estimating, and proposals in one end-to-end workflow. Trained on 10M+ residential properties. Instant Design: AI-generated photo-realistic home visualizations.
Visual proof: contractors can see the AI output

Audit insight: Outcome framing outperforms technology framing, users respond to "save 4 hours" more than "powered by AI." Roofr's data depth is an unspoken credibility asset none of the audited competitors are fully leveraging.

Key early findings

60%
Said knowing they could review AI output before anything was sent or saved would be the most confidence-building feature, nearly double the next answer. The clearest signal in the dataset.
65%
Cite inaccurate output as their top AI concern, nearly double the next concern. In a high-stakes industry where an estimate error costs real money, accuracy is the trust contract.
57%
Want to know when AI is involved: 40% always, 19% specifically for high-stakes tasks like estimates or customer communications. Only 4% said it doesn't matter.
54%
Have used AI tools outside their business software in the last six months, mostly ChatGPT. Open to AI, but haven't found it embedded in their roofing workflow yet.
2.9/5
Average comfort with AI tools, right at neutral, leaning slightly cautious. The industry isn't resistant, but it isn't enthusiastic either. It's waiting to be shown something that works.

Validated design direction: "Reviewed by AI, confirmed by you." 60% of respondents named human review before send/save as the single most confidence-building feature. This isn't an edge case preference: it's the majority position, and it applies platform-wide.

Three threads to watch

01
Pain and AI use are aligned. Estimates and admin are where contractors feel the most friction, and where early adopters are already experimenting. This validates them as the right starting points for AI features.
02
Transparency is non-negotiable. "Reviewed by AI, confirmed by you" is the right design direction for the majority of users. Accuracy failure on a high-stakes output will undo adoption faster than any onboarding can rebuild it.
03
The bar to try is low-cost and low-stakes. Contractors are open but cautious. Features that are free to start, reviewable before action, and proven on something small first will get adoption. Features that ask for trust upfront won't.
Strategic Reach

This research is shaping the AI product strategy

The output of this research isn't a feature spec: it's an opportunity map, personas, and a risk assessment that every team designing AI features will work from. It answers the questions that can't be answered by looking at metrics: how do contractors think about AI, and what would it take for them to trust it?

The competitor audit identified XBuild as an urgent competitive threat and flagged that Roofr's data depth is a credibility asset currently being undersold. These are strategic inputs to roadmap conversations, not just UX findings.

PM-level work in practice

Designing the research methodology, the AI Readiness segmentation framework, the survey instrument, the recruitment targeting, required thinking about what decisions the research needed to enable, not just what questions to ask. That's a product management framing applied to a research context.

The "over-transparency vs under-transparency" framing is now a shared mental model across the product and growth teams for evaluating how to communicate AI features.
