Progressive Proposals Setup

Role Designer & Roadmap Lead
Scope Activation, Proposals, Onboarding
Type Multi-project initiative
Context

New users weren't failing at proposals. They were failing before proposals.

Roofr's proposals product worked well for users who got there. The problem was that most new users never got there — and the product wasn't surfacing why.

The activation pattern: users would land on proposals, encounter an empty state, and either start the setup process or stall. The setup process required a fully configured catalog, at least one template, and a supplier connection before you could send your first proposal. Users who completed all three sent proposals. Users who didn't, didn't.

The insight: The bottleneck wasn't a UX problem inside proposals — it was a prerequisite problem that existed before users ever opened proposals. Improving the proposal creation flow wouldn't move activation. Removing the setup wall would.

Three root causes of the setup wall

01
Perceived complexity
Catalog setup felt like a significant project with no immediate payoff visible. Users didn't know what they were working toward.
02
Prerequisite confusion
Users didn't understand why a catalog was required to send a proposal, or that the connection existed. The dependency was invisible.
03
Chicken-and-egg
Users wanted to see proposals working before investing time in catalog setup. But catalog setup was required to see proposals working.
Process

Roadmap reorganization first

The existing proposals roadmap was organized around features for active users: better templates, more proposal options, richer editing. All valuable — but wrong sequencing if the activation rate was the constraint.

The roadmap reorganization reordered the queue around a single question: what's the minimum we need to ship for a new user to successfully send their first proposal without completing full setup? That work moved to the front. Feature-depth work moved behind it.

This is a product management decision embedded in a design process. It required building alignment with the PM and engineering lead around a diagnosis — "the bottleneck is setup prerequisites, not proposal features" — before any design work started.

The unlock strategy

Instead of redesigning the setup flow, the solution was to make setup optional for first activation:

Roofr Jumpstart Catalog
Pre-populated catalog items that work out of the box. No setup required to get a working proposal with real line items.
Seeded Templates
Account-tailored proposal templates that are ready on signup. First proposal in minutes, not after a setup session.
Flexible Pathways
New entry points to proposals that don't require supplier connection or completed catalog as prerequisites.
Questionnaire-Driven Setup
A tailored setup flow that adapts to account type. Surfaces the most relevant path, not a one-size-fits-all checklist.

Many small projects, one large unlock

No single change removes the activation wall. The initiative is a suite of interconnected smaller projects — each one removes a specific prerequisite or friction point — that together move the activation metric. Prioritization, sequencing, and dependency mapping are ongoing parts of the work.

Strategic Reach

A diagnosis before a design

The strategic contribution here wasn't the individual designs — it was identifying that the proposals activation problem was upstream of proposals. That diagnosis changed what got built and in what order.

Framing the problem as "prerequisites block activation" rather than "proposals UX is hard" led to a completely different solution set. The roadmap reorganization was the direct result of that reframe.

Pattern that scales

The prerequisite-blocking pattern isn't unique to proposals. Several other Roofr features require significant setup before first use. The progressive setup approach — seeded defaults, flexible pathways, optional full configuration — is a pattern that could apply to onboarding for any setup-heavy feature.

This is an example of a design system decision that lives above any individual feature: how should Roofr handle setup friction across the platform?
